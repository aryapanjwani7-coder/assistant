① DOPPLER EFFECT · BEATS review p.1 · lect. 7

%%DOPPLER%%
His review-slide form: put an x-axis pointing from source → observer; \(v_s, v_o\) = signed velocity components along it, relative to the ground (still air):
\(f'=\dfrac{v-v_o}{v-v_s}\,f\)   v = speed of sound (343 m/s at 20 °C)
Source toward observer: \(v_s>0\) ⇒ f′↑. Observer toward source: \(v_o<0\) ⇒ f′↑.

Memory form \(f'=f\,\dfrac{v\pm v_o}{v\mp v_s}\) with magnitudes: upper sign = moving TOWARD the other, lower = away. "Toward ⇒ f′ higher" always.

caseλ′f′
source approaching\(\frac{v-v_s}{f}\) squeezed\(\frac{v}{v-v_s}f\) higher
source receding\(\frac{v+v_s}{f}\) stretched\(\frac{v}{v+v_s}f\) reduced
observer approachingλ unchanged\(\frac{v+v_o}{v}f\)
observer recedingλ unchanged\(\frac{v-v_o}{v}f\)

Moving source changes λ (wavefronts bunch up). A moving observer changes how fast wavefronts are met, so the relative speed changes, not λ.

Slow speeds (\(v_s,v_o\ll v\)): \(\frac{\Delta f}{f}\approx-\frac{v_r}{v}\), \(v_r=v_o-v_s\) (positive = separating).

His 3 follow-up questions

  • Car moving away? f′ is reduced: \(f'=\frac{v}{v+v_s}f\).
  • Reflected by a wall? A stationary wall keeps the frequency it receives. Moving reflector (speed u toward a stationary source/listener) = double Doppler: the wall hears \(f_1=f\frac{v+u}{v}\), then re-emits as a moving source: \(f_2=f_1\frac{v}{v-u}=f\frac{v+u}{v-u}\). Beats with the original: \(|f_2-f|\).
  • Wind? The speed of sound is modified: relative to the ground it travels at \(v\pm w\) (downwind +). Work in the air frame (velocities relative to the air, v unchanged), or use \(f'=\frac{v+w-v_o}{v+w-v_s}f\) with w along the source→observer axis. Source and observer both at rest ⇒ no shift (f′ = f); only λ and travel time change.
Beats (at a fixed position): \(f_{beat}=|f_1-f_2|\)
\(y=2A\cos\!\big(2\pi\tfrac{f_1-f_2}{2}t\big)\cos(2\pi f_{avg}t)\), \(f_{avg}=\frac{f_1+f_2}{2}\). You hear pitch \(f_{avg}\) with loudness pulsing \(|f_1-f_2|\) times/s (e.g. 500 & 505 Hz give 5 beats/s, not a 5 Hz tone). The envelope has frequency Δf/2, but loudness peaks twice per envelope cycle, so beats = Δf. Piano tuner: 440 vs 442 gives 2 beats/s, and beats slow as the notes match.

Doppler + beats combo: e.g. a moving source with a stationary one at the same f: \(f_{beat}=|f'-f|\). Two listeners or a reflector: find each f′ separately, then subtract.

Light: receding gives \(\Delta\lambda=\lambda_{obs}-\lambda_{src}>0\) (redshift); approaching gives blueshift (Andromeda). Hubble \(v=H_0d\), age \(\approx1/H_0\approx14\) Gyr. Standard candle \(F=\frac{L}{4\pi d^2}\Rightarrow d=\sqrt{\frac{L}{4\pi F}}\). Binary stars and exoplanets: periodic shifts.

Shock wave: Mach \(M=\frac{v_{jet}}{c_s}\). Cone: \(\sin\theta=\frac{c_st}{v_{jet}t}=\frac{1}{M}\) (M = 2 gives θ = 30°). The boom is heard whenever the cone passes you, continuously, not only when "breaking the barrier".

① cont. · SOUND · INTENSITY · PIPES lect. 7

Sound is a longitudinal pressure wave in air and fluids (no shear); solids carry both kinds. \(s=s_{max}\cos(kx\mp\omega t+\varphi)\), \(\Delta P=\Delta P_{max}\sin(kx\mp\omega t+\varphi)\): 90° out of phase with s. Hearing 20 Hz–20 kHz.

Speed \(=\sqrt{\frac{\text{restoring}}{\text{inertia}}}\): fluid \(\sqrt{B/\rho}\), solid \(\sqrt{Y/\rho}\), ideal gas \(v=\sqrt{\gamma RT_K/M}\) (adiabatic \(pV^\gamma\)=const gives \(dp/d\rho=\gamma p/\rho\)). Air: 331 m/s at 0 °C, 343 at 20 °C, \(v\approx331\sqrt{T_K/273}\). \(B=-\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta V/V}\), \(Y=\frac{F/A}{\Delta L/L}\). Faster in solids. Sound bends toward the slower medium (SOFAR channel).

Intensity \(I=\frac{P}{4\pi r^2}\) (2r gives I/4, −6 dB). \(I=\frac{(\Delta p_{max})^2}{2\rho v}=\tfrac12\Delta p_{max}u_{max}\), \(u_{max}=\frac{\Delta p_{max}}{\rho v}=\omega s_{max}\). \(I\propto A^2\).

Level \(\beta=10\log_{10}\frac{I}{I_0}\) dB, \(I_0=10^{-12}\) W/m² (threshold), pain ≈ 1 W/m² = 120 dB. 10×I adds 10 dB; 2×I adds 3 dB; N equal sources add \(10\log N\). \(I=I_0 10^{\beta/10}\). ~1 dB is just noticeable; 3 dB is easily noticed. Loudness (phon) also depends on f.

Interference (two speakers in phase): \(\Delta r=|r_2-r_1|=n\lambda\) is constructive; \(\Delta r=(n+\tfrac12)\lambda\) is destructive. Phase φ = 2πΔr/λ, \(A_R=2A|\cos\frac\varphi2|\). Noise cancelling: anti-sound at 180°.

%%PIPES%%

Open–open: \(\lambda_n=\frac{2L}{n},\ f_n=\frac{nv}{2L}\), n = 1, 2, 3… (slide says "1, 3, 5", but its own picture shows λ = 2L, L, 2L/3: all n). Closed–open: \(\lambda_n=\frac{4L}{n},\ f_n=\frac{nv}{4L}\), odd n only. String fixed–fixed: \(f_n=\frac{n}{2L}\sqrt{F_T/\mu}\). Vocal folds \(f\sim\frac1{2L}\sqrt{F_T/\mu}\) (shorter and lighter give higher f). Timbre = the mix of harmonics \(f_1,2f_1,3f_1…\); resonance means driving at a natural frequency.

② H-ATOM: ORBIT · ESCAPE · 3D vs 2D review p.2

Proton +e, electron −e, \(r=5.29\times10^{-11}\) m. Electric force is the centripetal force; ignore gravity.

\(F=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r^2}=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(1.602\times10^{-19})^2}{(5.29\times10^{-11})^2}=\) 8.25×10⁻⁸ N (attractive). Gravity \(F_G=G\frac{m_em_p}{r^2}=3.63\times10^{-47}\) N. Ratio ≈ 2×10³⁹.

  1. Orbit speed: \(\frac{m_ev^2}{r}=\frac{ke^2}{r^2}\Rightarrow\) \(v=\sqrt{\frac{ke^2}{m_er}}\) \(=\) 2.19×10⁶ m/s. Period \(T=\frac{2\pi r}{v}=1.52\times10^{-16}\) s.
  2. Energies (U(∞)=0): \(U=-\frac{ke^2}{r}=-27.2\) eV, \(K=\tfrac12m_ev^2=\frac{ke^2}{2r}=+13.6\) eV, \(E=K+U=-\frac{ke^2}{2r}=\) −13.6 eV (bound, E<0). Ionization energy 13.6 eV.
  3. Escape: reach ∞ with zero speed, \(E_i=E_f=0\): \(\tfrac12m_ev_{esc}^2-\frac{ke^2}{r}=0\Rightarrow\) \(v_{esc}=\sqrt{\frac{2ke^2}{m_er}}\) \(=\sqrt2\,v_{orb}=\) 3.10×10⁶ m/s. Same structure as gravity's \(v_{esc}=\sqrt{2GM/r}\) (swap GMm for ke²). To escape you need KE larger than |PE|.
2D world: F ∝ 1/r (given: \(F_r=-\frac{C}{r}\), C = "κe²" > 0). Why: field lines spread over a circumference \(2\pi r\), not an area \(4\pi r^2\) (same as an infinite line charge, \(E=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0 r}\)).
  1. \(F=-\frac{dU}{dr}\Rightarrow\frac{dU}{dr}=\frac{C}{r}\). Integrate: \(U(r)=C\ln r+\text{const}\). Choose U(r₀) = 0 (any reference): \(U(r)=C\ln\frac{r}{r_0}\) (ln r − ln r₀ = ln(r/r₀))
  2. Start at r₀ with v₀, stop at max separation R (K = 0): \(\tfrac12m_ev_0^2=U(R)-U(r_0)=C\ln\frac{R}{r_0}\)
  3. \(v_0=\sqrt{\frac{2C}{m_e}\ln\frac{R}{r_0}}\). As R→∞, \(\ln(R/r_0)\to\infty\), so \(v_{escape}\to\infty\). No finite escape velocity in 2D.
U grows without bound as r→∞ (in 3D U→0). Any finite energy has a turning point where K = E − U = 0. 2D circular orbit: \(\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{C}{r}\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{C/m}\), the same at every r! C has different units from 3D k; use the model given.

③ VAN DE GRAAFF · HOLLOW METAL SPHERE review p.3

Belt carries charge up to a hollow conducting dome (radius R, charge Q). The prof confirmed: metal sphere only, not uniformly charged plastic. Charge sits on the outer surface.

Gauss: imagine a sphere of radius r: \(E\cdot4\pi r^2=\frac{Q_{enc}}{\epsilon_0}\). r < R: \(Q_{enc}=0\Rightarrow E=0\). r > R: \(E=\frac{kQ}{r^2}\), like a point charge.

Potential (V(∞)=0), \(V=-\int_\infty^rE\,dr\):
r ≥ R: \(V=\frac{kQ}{r}\)
r ≤ R: \(V=\frac{kQ}{R}\) = const ≠ 0
Surface: \(E(R^+)=\frac{kQ}{R^2}=\frac{V}{R}=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\)

%%SPHERE%%
"E = 0 inside but V = 100 kV?" \(E_r=-\frac{dV}{dr}=0\) means V is constant, not zero. Its value is fixed by continuity with the outside at r = R. E depends on the gradient of V, not its value.
Lecture example: diameter 25.0 cm, so R = 0.125 m, V = 100 kV.
  1. Charge: \(Q=4\pi\epsilon_0RV=\frac{RV}{k}=\frac{(0.125)(10^5)}{8.99\times10^9}=\) 1.39 μC (small Q, yet huge V)
  2. Surface field: \(E=\frac VR=\frac{10^5}{0.125}=\) 8.0×10⁵ V/m < 3×10⁶ (no breakdown). σ = Q/4πR² = 7.08×10⁻⁶ C/m².
  3. Max charge before air breaks down (\(E_{bd}\approx3\times10^6\) V/m): \(\frac{kQ_{max}}{R^2}=E_{bd}\Rightarrow Q_{max}=\frac{E_{bd}R^2}{k}=\) 5.2 μC, \(V_{max}=E_{bd}R=\) 375 kV.
  4. Work to bring q from ∞ to the surface (slowly): \(W_{ext}=q\,\Delta V=q\frac{kQ}{R}\) (field does \(-qV\)). Example: q = 1 μC at 100 kV gives 0.1 J. Released from the surface, +q gains \(K=qV\) going to ∞.
  5. Outside: at r = 2R, V = 50 kV, E = 2×10⁵ V/m; V(∞) = 0.

Scaling: fixed Q, 2R gives V/2, E/4. Fixed V, 2R gives 2Q, E/2. Capacitance \(C=4\pi\epsilon_0R=R/k=13.9\) pF. Energy \(U=\tfrac12QV=\tfrac12CV^2=\) 0.0695 J (slide prints 0.695 J, a typo).

Charge q placed inside the cavity (★): −q is induced on the inner wall and +q added to the outer surface, so E ≠ 0 in the cavity but still 0 in the metal. Faraday cage: an empty closed metal shell has E = 0 inside whatever the outside field (grounding not needed). Plastic uniformly charged sphere (★, not tested): inside \(E=\frac{kQr}{R^3}\), \(V=\frac{kQ(3R^2-r^2)}{2R^3}\).

Sharp points: small sphere \(E=\frac VR\). At fixed V, smaller R gives a bigger E (corona, lightning rods). Needle R = 0.1 mm: breakdown at \(V=E_{bd}R=300\) V.

④ GAUSS'S LAW · CUBE FLUX review p.4 · lect. 10

Flux (uniform E, flat surface): \(\Phi_E=\vec E\cdot\vec A=EA\cos\theta\), θ measured from the normal (not the plane!). General \(\Phi=\int\vec E\cdot\hat n\,dA\). Closed surface: normal points outward. Out is +, in is −, parallel to the face is 0. Units N·m²/C.

Gauss: \(\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec A=\frac{Q_{enc}}{\epsilon_0}\). The surface must be closed and orientable (a Klein bottle won't do). \(Q_{enc}\) is the algebraic sum inside. Outside charges change the local E on the surface but add zero net flux.

Pattern: charge at centre/face/edge/corner shares 1/1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 of \(Q/\epsilon_0\). Why 0 on the near faces: the field from a corner charge points radially, i.e. along the 3 planes that meet at the corner, so \(\vec E\cdot\hat n=0\).

Lecture examples: 10 N/C at 30° above the xy-plane, through 6 m² in the xz-plane (n = ŷ): \(\Phi=EA\cos30°=52\) N·m²/C. \(\vec E=cy^2\hat k\) through an a×b rectangle in xy, strips dA = b dy: \(\Phi=cb\int_0^ay^2dy=\tfrac13a^3bc\). Blobs: count only the charges inside, e.g. −4+6−1 = +1 μC.

Strategy + standard results

Symmetry → surface where E is constant and ∥ or ⊥ to n̂. Sphere \(E\,4\pi r^2\) · cylinder \(E\,2\pi rL\) · pillbox \(2EA\).

  • Uniform insulating sphere ρ₀: in \(E=\frac{kQr}{R^3}\) (∝ r), out \(\frac{kQ}{r^2}\); max at R. \(\rho=ar^n\): \(Q_{enc}=\frac{4\pi a}{n+3}r^{n+3}\), in \(E=\frac{ar^{n+1}}{(n+3)\epsilon_0}\), out \(\frac{aR^{n+3}}{(n+3)\epsilon_0r^2}\).
  • Cylindrical shell σ₀: out \(E=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0r}=\frac{R\sigma_0}{\epsilon_0r}\), in 0. Infinite plane \(\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\) (independent of distance). Two opposite planes: \(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\) between, 0 outside.
  • Conductor (electrostatic equilibrium): E = 0 inside the metal and in an empty cavity (Faraday cage, no ground needed); excess charge on the outer surface; just outside \(E=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}\), ⊥ to the surface; the whole conductor is equipotential.
  • Differential form \(\nabla\cdot\vec E=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}\) (divergence theorem). Zero divergence ≠ zero E (a uniform field has div 0).

⑤ ELECTRIC DIPOLE review p.5 · lect. 9, 11

Exactly two charges +q and −q (prof: "no more, no less"), separation d. \(\vec p=q\vec d\), pointing from − to +. Units C·m.

%%DIPOLE%%
Field on the perpendicular bisector at height z (charges at x = ±d/2):
  1. Each charge: \(|E_\pm|=\frac{kq}{r^2}\), \(r=\sqrt{z^2+(d/2)^2}\)
  2. Geometry: \(\cos\theta=\frac zr\), \(\sin\theta=\frac{d/2}{r}\) (θ from the z-axis)
  3. z-components cancel; x-components add: \(E=2\frac{kq}{r^2}\sin\theta\)
  4. \(E=\dfrac{kqd}{(z^2+d^2/4)^{3/2}}=\dfrac{kp}{(z^2+d^2/4)^{3/2}}\), direction opposite p (parallel to the line from + to −)
  5. z ≫ d: \(E\approx\frac{kp}{z^3}\), falls off faster than a point charge (net charge 0)

Compare: two equal +q: x-parts cancel, \(E_z=\frac{2kq\,z}{r^3}\to\frac{k(2q)}{z^2}\) (point charge 2q). On the axis (distance z from the centre): \(E=\frac{2kpz}{(z^2-d^2/4)^2}\to\frac{2kp}{z^3}\), along p.

Potential (scalar, no components!): \(V=kq\big(\frac{1}{r_+}-\frac{1}{r_-}\big)\), \(r_\pm=\sqrt{x^2+(z\mp\frac d2)^2}\). Far: \(V=\frac{k\,\vec p\cdot\hat r}{r^2}=\frac{kp\cos\theta}{r^2}\). On the bisector: V = 0 but E ≠ 0.
Lecture: q = 3 nC, d = 4 cm: V(0,0,1 cm) = 1.80×10³ V; V(0,0,−5 cm) = −514 V; V(3,0,2 cm) = 360 V.

Dipole in a uniform external E: \(\vec F_+=q\vec E\), \(\vec F_-=-q\vec E\), so net force 0, but the forces are separated, giving a torque: \(\vec\tau=\frac{\vec d}{2}\times q\vec E+(-\frac{\vec d}{2})\times(-q\vec E)=\) \(\vec p\times\vec E\), \(|\tau|=pE\sin\theta\)
Energy: \(dU=-dW=pE\sin\theta\,d\theta\Rightarrow\) \(U=-\vec p\cdot\vec E=-pE\cos\theta\) (U = 0 at 90°). \(\tau_\theta=-\frac{dU}{d\theta}\) (like \(F=-\frac{dU}{dx}\)).

Work by an external agent to rotate θ₁→θ₂: \(W=U_2-U_1=pE(\cos\theta_1-\cos\theta_2)\). Small oscillations: \(I\ddot\theta=-pE\theta\Rightarrow\omega=\sqrt{pE/I}\).
Example: q = 2 μC, d = 0.10 m, so p = 2×10⁻⁷ C·m. E = 5×10⁴ N/C, θ = 30°: τ = pE sin30° = 5.0×10⁻³ N·m, U = −pE cos30° = −8.7×10⁻³ J.
Shifted / asymmetric charges: never assume cancellation. For each charge: \(\vec r=\) (P − source), \(E_x=\frac{kq_i\,\Delta x}{r^3}\), \(E_y=\frac{kq_i\,\Delta y}{r^3}\) (sign of q included), then add components: \(|E|=\sqrt{E_x^2+E_y^2}\), \(\theta=\tan^{-1}\frac{E_y}{E_x}\).

Non-uniform E also gives a net force. Induced dipole: an atom polarizes in E. Water is a permanent dipole. Microwaves: lagging polarization absorbs \(\langle p\rangle=\tfrac12\omega E_0P_0\sin\delta\). Flocking: \(q\vec E\) moves fibres, \(\vec p\times\vec E\) stands them up.

★ CHARGE · COULOMB · FIELDS lect. 8–9

Charge is quantized (q = ne, e = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C; quarks ±⅓e, ±⅔e) and conserved, locally too: \(\frac{dQ_{in}}{dt}=-I_{out}\). Conductors have mobile electrons; insulators have bound ones. A charged comb attracts neutral paper by polarization (near charges closer). Induction: rod near a sphere → ground it → cut the ground → remove the rod: the sphere keeps the opposite charge.

Coulomb \(\vec F_{12}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1q_2}{r_{12}^2}\hat r_{12}\) (on 1 from 2; like charges repel). Newton's 3rd law holds. Both Coulomb and gravity go as 1/r² because "flux" spreads over 4πr² (3D). Superposition: add vectors.
Ex: q₂ = −3e at the origin, q₁ = +2e at (0, d), q₃ = −5e at (2d, 0): \(F_{21}=\frac{k6e^2}{d^2}\) (+y), \(F_{23}=\frac{k15e^2}{4d^2}\) (−x), \(F=\sqrt{F_{21}^2+F_{23}^2}\), \(\theta=\tan^{-1}\frac{F_{21}}{F_{23}}\).

Field \(\vec E=\vec F/q\), \(\vec E=\sum\frac{kq_i}{r_i^2}\hat r_i\): away from +, toward −. He⁺ example: E = k(2e)/r² = 4.1×10¹² N/C at 26.5 pm. Field lines start on + and end on −, number ∝ q, tangent = E, density = |E|, never cross.

★ POTENTIAL · ENERGY · APPLICATIONS lect. 11

Conservative: \(\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec l=0\) (path-independent). \(W_e=-\Delta U\), \(V=\frac Uq\), \(\Delta U=q\Delta V\), \(V_B-V_A=-\int_A^B\vec E\cdot d\vec l\). Two-charge energy \(U=\frac{kQq}{r}\) (property of the system; >0 for like charges). Point \(V=\frac{kq}{r}\); many charges \(V=\sum\frac{kq_i}{r_i}\) (scalar sum).

Energy conservation: \(K_i+qV_i=K_f+qV_f\). Through ΔV from rest: \(\tfrac12mv^2=|q\Delta V|\) (electron through 100 V: 5.93×10⁶ m/s). 1 eV = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ J. 3 nC pushed by a fixed 5 nC from 10 to 15 cm: \(K=kQq(\frac1{r_1}-\frac1{r_2})=4.5\times10^{-7}\) J.
Assembly work \(W=\sum_{i<j}\frac{kq_iq_j}{r_{ij}}\) (order doesn't matter). Square of 2, 3, 4, 5 μC with 1 cm sides: W₂ = 5.4, W₃ = 15.9, W₄ = 36.5, total 57.8 J.

E from V: \(\vec E=-\nabla V\), \(E_r=-\frac{dV}{dr}\). E points toward decreasing V; V = 0 does not mean E = 0. Poisson \(\nabla^2V=-\rho/\epsilon_0\). Equipotentials ⊥ E, and W = 0 along them. Uniform E (plates): \(V_A-V_B=Ed\), \(E=\frac{|\Delta V|}{d}\); 1 N/C = 1 V/m. Plates with σ = 6.81×10⁻⁷: E = σ/ε₀ = 7.69×10⁴ V/m, ΔV = 500 V at 6.5 mm. Max for 2.5 cm of air: 3×10⁶ × 0.025 = 7.5×10⁴ V. Battery: q = ΔU/ΔV; a 30 W lamp at 12 V passes 2.5 C/s = 1.56×10¹⁹ e/s.

Millikan: \(|q|E=mg\Rightarrow|q|=\frac{mg}{E}=\frac{mgd}{V}\); charges come in multiples of e. Inkjet: \(a_y=\frac{qE}{m}\), \(t=\frac{L}{v_0}\), \(y=\frac{qE}{2mv_0^2}x^2\) (example: 1.0×10³ m/s², 1 ms, 0.50 mm). Precipitator: \(|q|E=6\pi\eta rv_d\Rightarrow v_d=\frac{|q|E}{6\pi\eta r}\). Photocopier: light discharges the drum; toner sticks to charged (CAD) or exposed (DAD) areas. Spray paint wraps around; Faraday recesses are poorly coated.
