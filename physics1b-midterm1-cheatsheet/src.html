① MEASURING DENSITY · BUOYANCY review p.2 · ch.1

Fluid statics basics

\(\rho=\frac{m}{V}\), \(P=\frac{F}{A}\) (Pa = N/m²). At rest: \(P=P_0+\rho g h\). Depends only on depth, not on container shape. Gauge \(=P-P_{atm}\).

Pascal: a pressure change spreads to the whole fluid. Hydraulic lift: \(\frac{F_1}{A_1}=\frac{F_2}{A_2}\) but \(d_2=d_1\frac{A_1}{A_2}\), so work in = work out.

Archimedes: \(F_B=\rho_{fl}V_{disp}\,g\) = weight of displaced fluid. Where it comes from: \(F_B=(P_{bot}-P_{top})A=\rho_{fl} g h A\). It does not depend on depth or on what the object is made of.

Weighing method (lecture: air vs. submerged)

(a) in air: \(T=w=\rho_{obj}V_{obj}\,g\)   (b) submerged (\(V_{disp}=V_{obj}\)): \(T=w_{app}=w-F_B\)
\(\Rightarrow F_B=w-w_{app}=\rho_{fl}V g\). Divide (a)/(this):
\(\dfrac{\rho_{obj}}{\rho_{fl}}=\dfrac{w}{w-w_{app}}\)   also \(V=\dfrac{w-w_{app}}{\rho_{fl}g}\)
Unknown liquid: weigh one object in water and in the liquid: \(\frac{\rho_{liq}}{\rho_{w}}=\frac{w-w_{app,liq}}{w-w_{app,w}}\).
★ His slide question: "What if we DON'T ignore air density?" Air also buoys the object:
\(w_{air}=(\rho_{obj}-\rho_{air})Vg\),   \(w_{liq}=(\rho_{obj}-\rho_{fl})Vg\)
Subtract: \(w_{air}-w_{liq}=(\rho_{fl}-\rho_{air})Vg\). Divide: \(\dfrac{\rho_{obj}-\rho_{air}}{\rho_{fl}-\rho_{air}}=\dfrac{w_{air}}{w_{air}-w_{liq}}\)
\(\rho_{obj}=\rho_{air}+(\rho_{fl}-\rho_{air})\dfrac{w_{air}}{w_{air}-w_{liq}}\)
Check: \(\rho_{air}\!\to\!0\) gives the lecture result. Error \(=\rho_{air}(1-\tfrac{w}{w-w_{app}})<0\), so ignoring air slightly overestimates \(\rho_{obj}\). It's tiny, since \(\rho_{air}\approx1.2\) vs \(\rho_w=1000\) kg/m³ (~0.1%).

Floating & FBD tricks

  • Floats: \(F_B=w \Rightarrow \frac{V_{sub}}{V}=\frac{\rho_{obj}}{\rho_{fl}}\) (ice 0.92, so ~90% of an iceberg is under). Sinks iff \(\rho_{avg}>\rho_{fl}\). Hollow: \(\rho_{avg}=m_{tot}/V_{tot}\). Two layers: \(\rho_{obj}V=\rho_1V_1+\rho_2V_2\).
  • Object hung in a beaker on a scale: \(T+F_B=mg\). The scale reads \(W_{beaker+water}+F_B\) (Newton's 3rd law: object pushes the water down).
  • Melting ice: level unchanged. Rock tossed from boat: level falls.

② FLOW RATE · CONTINUITY · BERNOULLI review p.3

Volume flow rate \(Q=\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{d}{dt}(Ax)=A\frac{dx}{dt}=Av\) [m³/s]. Mass flow \(\dot m=\rho Av\).

Continuity (incompressible): \(Q_1=Q_2\Rightarrow A_1v_1=A_2v_2\) for any points 1, 2. \(v\propto 1/A\propto 1/d^2\): halve d gives 4× v. Branches: \(Q_{in}=\sum Q_{out}\).

Bernoulli (incompressible, frictionless/non-viscous, steady, along a streamline):
\(P+\tfrac12\rho v^2+\rho g y=\text{const}\)
Each term is an energy per volume (J/m³ = Pa): pressure + KE density + PE density.

Derivation (work–energy process): push a slug \(dV\) through: \(W=P_1A_1dx_1-P_2A_2dx_2=(P_1-P_2)dV\) (continuity: \(A_1dx_1=A_2dx_2=dV\)). \(\Delta K=\tfrac12\rho dV(v_2^2-v_1^2)\), \(\Delta U=\rho dV g(y_2-y_1)\). Then \(W=\Delta K+\Delta U \Rightarrow P_1-P_2=\tfrac12\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)+\rho g(y_2-y_1)\).

Limits: v=0 gives statics \(P=P_0+\rho gh\). Same height: faster means lower P (Venturi).

Applications

  • Torricelli (open tank, hole depth h, \(A_{tank}\gg A_{hole}\)): \(P_1=P_2=P_{atm}\), \(v_1\approx0\) give \(v=\sqrt{2gh}\) (free fall). \(Q=A_h\sqrt{2gh}\). Hole at height H: range \(2\sqrt{hH}\).
  • Venturi (horizontal, \(A_1>A_2\)): \(P_1-P_2=\tfrac12\rho v_1^2\big[(\tfrac{A_1}{A_2})^2-1\big]\). Manometer: \(P_1-P_2=(\rho_m-\rho)gh\).
  • Prandtl/Pitot tube (disc. wk 2): front opening 1 is a dead spot, \(v_1=0\). Side opening 2 sees air at speed \(v_2\). Bernoulli: \(P_1-P_2=\tfrac12\rho_{air}v_2^2\). U-tube: \(\rho g h=P_1-P_2\) gives \(h=\frac{\rho_{air}v_2^2}{2\rho g}\), so \(v_2=\sqrt{\frac{2\rho g h}{\rho_{air}}}\).
  • Lift: faster air on top means lower P on top: \(F\approx\tfrac12\rho(v_t^2-v_b^2)A\) (roofs in storms, curveball).
  • Siphon: exit speed \(\sqrt{2gd}\) (d = drop from surface to exit). Pipe rising at constant A: v is constant, so P drops by \(\rho g\Delta y\).
Viscous (Bernoulli fails): \(Q=\frac{\pi R^4\Delta P}{8\eta L}\) (R/2 gives Q/16). Turbulent if \(Re=\frac{\rho vD}{\eta}\gtrsim2000\).

★ SHM & PENDULUMS ch.2 · disc. wk 2

\(F=-kx\Rightarrow\ddot x=-\frac{k}{m}x\Rightarrow\) \(x=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)\), \(\omega=\sqrt{k/m}=2\pi f=\frac{2\pi}{T}\).
\(v=-A\omega\sin(\omega t+\phi)\), \(a=-\omega^2x\). \(v_{max}=A\omega\) (at x=0), \(a_{max}=A\omega^2\) (at ±A), \(v(x)=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}\).

ICs: \(A=\sqrt{x_0^2+(v_0/\omega)^2}\), \(\tan\phi=-\frac{v_0}{\omega x_0}\). Energy \(E=\tfrac12kA^2=\tfrac12mv^2+\tfrac12kx^2=\tfrac12mv_{max}^2\). KE = PE at \(x=A/\sqrt2\).

Vertical spring: same ω, equilibrium shifted by mg/k. Springs in parallel: \(k_1+k_2\); in series: \(\frac1k=\frac1{k_1}+\frac1{k_2}\).

Simple pendulum: \(\tau=-mgL\sin\theta=I\alpha\), \(I=mL^2\), so \(\ddot\theta=-\frac gL\sin\theta\approx-\frac gL\theta\) (small angle). \(\omega=\sqrt{g/L},\ T=2\pi\sqrt{L/g}\), independent of m and A. Measure g: \(g=4\pi^2L/T^2\). For better precision: long L, time many periods, small amplitude. Clock: \(\frac{\Delta T}{T}=\frac12\frac{\Delta L}{L}\), so 2% too long means 1% slow ≈ 14.4 min/day.

Physical pendulum (d = pivot→CM): \(I\ddot\theta=-mgd\sin\theta\), so \(\omega=\sqrt{\frac{mgd}{I}},\ T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}}\). Rod pivoted at end: \(T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}\).
I: rod (center) \(\frac{ML^2}{12}\), rod (end) \(\frac{ML^2}{3}\), hoop \(MR^2\), disk \(\frac12MR^2\), sphere \(\frac25MR^2\), shell \(\frac23MR^2\). Parallel axis: \(I=I_{cm}+Md^2\).

Torsional: \(\tau=-\kappa\theta\), \(\omega=\sqrt{\kappa/I}\), \(T=2\pi\sqrt{I/\kappa}\) (κ in N·m/rad).

④ DAMPED OSCILLATIONS: THE FULL PROCESS review p.5 · lect. 4 · "follow the process"

Setup: drag \(F=-bv\) (b in kg/s). Newton: \(ma=-bv-kx\), so

\(m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=0\) (1)  ⟺  \(\ddot x+2\gamma\dot x+\omega_0^2x=0\)

\(\gamma\equiv\frac{b}{2m}\) (decay rate, 1/s), \(\omega_0\equiv\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}\) (natural ang. freq.). Damping ratio \(\zeta=\frac{\gamma}{\omega_0}=\frac{b}{2\sqrt{mk}}\).

Which case? Compare \(b^2\) vs \(4mk\) ⟺ \(\gamma\) vs \(\omega_0\) ⟺ \(b\) vs \(2\sqrt{mk}\).

A · UNDERDAMPED derivation (lecture)

  1. Try \(x=A_0e^{-rt}\cos(\omega t+\phi)\). Let \(\theta=\omega t+\phi\).
  2. \(\dot x=-A_0e^{-rt}(r\cos\theta+\omega\sin\theta)\)
  3. \(\ddot x=A_0e^{-rt}[(r^2-\omega^2)\cos\theta+2r\omega\sin\theta]\)
  4. Plug into (1): \(A_0e^{-rt}\{[m(r^2-\omega^2)-br+k]\cos\theta\) \(+(2mr-b)\,\omega\sin\theta\}=0\)
  5. True for all t, and cos θ, sin θ are independent, so each coefficient = 0:
    \(\sin\theta\): \(2mr-b=0\Rightarrow\) \(r=\frac{b}{2m}\)
    \(\cos\theta\): \(m(r^2-\omega^2)-br+k=0\) (2)
  6. Put r into (2): \(\omega^2=\frac{k}{m}-\frac{b^2}{4m^2}\). ω is real only if \(b^2<4mk\).
  7. \(x=\underbrace{A_0e^{-bt/2m}}_{\text{decaying amp.}}\underbrace{\cos(\omega t+\phi)}_{\text{oscillation}}\). Check b→0: SHM ✓.

B · OVERDAMPED derivation (lecture)

  1. Try \(x=A_0e^{-rt}\): \(\dot x=-rx\), \(\ddot x=r^2x\).
  2. Plug in: \((mr^2-br+k)A_0e^{-rt}=0\) (slide typo "kr")
    so \(mr^2-br+k=0\)
  3. Quadratic formula: \(r=\frac{b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4mk}}{2m}=\gamma\pm\alpha\)
  4. \(b^2>4mk\) gives two real roots \(r_1=\gamma+\alpha\) (fast), \(r_2=\gamma-\alpha\) (slow, dominates at late t). Both \(>0\) since \(\alpha<\gamma\), so x always decays.
  5. Linear ODE, so superpose: \(x=C_1e^{-r_1t}+C_2e^{-r_2t}\). \(r_1,r_2\) are the two decay time scales \(1/r_1,1/r_2\).
  6. Same thing: \(x=e^{-\gamma t}[C_1e^{-\alpha t}+C_2e^{\alpha t}]\) \(=e^{-\gamma t}[A\cosh\alpha t+B\frac{\sinh\alpha t}{\alpha}]\), with \(A=C_1+C_2\), \(B=\alpha(C_2-C_1)\).

C · CRITICAL (b²=4mk): "Not exactly!"

  1. \(r_1=r_2=r\), so \((C_1+C_2)e^{-rt}\) has only one constant. It can't match both x₀ and v₀, so we need a 2nd solution.
  2. From over: α→0 gives \(\cosh\alpha t\to1\), \(\frac{\sinh\alpha t}{\alpha}\to t\)
  3. From under: \(x=e^{-\gamma t}[A\cos\omega t+B\frac{\sin\omega t}{\omega}]\), ω→0 gives \(\cos\omega t\to1\), \(\frac{\sin\omega t}{\omega}\to t\) (slide typo "sinh/α")
  4. Both give \(x=(A+Bt)e^{-\gamma t}\), \(\gamma=\frac{b}{2m}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}\)
  5. Verify \(te^{-\gamma t}\): \(\dot x=(1-\gamma t)e^{-\gamma t}\), \(\ddot x=(\gamma^2t-2\gamma)e^{-\gamma t}\), so \(\ddot x+2\gamma\dot x+\gamma^2x\) \(=e^{-\gamma t}[\gamma^2t-2\gamma+2\gamma-2\gamma^2t+\gamma^2t]=0\) ✓
Initial conditions: one master pattern. \(x(0)=x_0,\ \dot x(0)=v_0\). Let \(B\equiv v_0+\gamma x_0\).
Under: \(x=e^{-\gamma t}\big[x_0\cos\omega t+\frac{B}{\omega}\sin\omega t\big]\)
Critical: \(x=e^{-\gamma t}\big[x_0+B\,t\big]\)
Over: \(x=e^{-\gamma t}\big[x_0\cosh\alpha t+\frac{B}{\alpha}\sinh\alpha t\big]\)
Why (lecture): \(x=e^{-\gamma t}[C\cos\omega t+D\sin\omega t]\). At t=0, \(C=x_0\). \(v(0)=-\gamma C+\omega D\Rightarrow D=\frac{v_0+\gamma x_0}{\omega}\), so D = (const)/ω and the ω→0 limit is finite. From rest (\(v_0=0\)): \(B=\gamma x_0\), e.g. critical \(x=x_0(1+\gamma t)e^{-\gamma t}\).
Amp–phase form (under): \(A_0=\sqrt{x_0^2+(B/\omega)^2}\), \(\tan\phi=-\frac{B}{\omega x_0}\).
Over, direct: \(C_1+C_2=x_0\), \(-r_1C_1-r_2C_2=v_0\), so \(C_1=-\frac{v_0+r_2x_0}{r_1-r_2}\), \(C_2=\frac{v_0+r_1x_0}{r_1-r_2}\).
Trend as b grows: ω falls and decay speeds up until critical. Past critical, return gets slower: \(r_2=\gamma-\alpha\to\frac{\omega_0^2}{2\gamma}=\frac{k}{b}\to0\). So critical is the fastest.
Any oscillator: swap in its \(\omega_0^2\) (g/L, mgd/I, κ/I) and \(2\gamma\) (= b/m).

④ cont. · ENERGY · Q · DRIVEN disc. wk 3 · lect. 4 p.5

Energy loss

\(E=\tfrac12m\dot x^2+\tfrac12kx^2\). Then \(\frac{dE}{dt}=\dot x(m\ddot x+kx)=\dot x(-b\dot x)\), so \(\frac{dE}{dt}=-b\dot x^2\le0\)

Weak damping (γ≪ω₀): \(E\approx\tfrac12kA_0^2e^{-2\gamma t}=E_0e^{-bt/m}\). Energy decays twice as fast as amplitude: \(\tau_E=\frac{m}{b}\), \(\tau_A=\frac{2m}{b}\).

Disc. wk 3, E(t) for the CRITICAL case: \(x=e^{-\alpha t}(A_0+A_1t)\), α = b/2m.
\(\dot x=e^{-\alpha t}[-\alpha(A_0+A_1t)+A_1]\). Critical means \(\alpha^2=\frac{k}{m}\), so \(E=\tfrac12m(\dot x^2+\alpha^2x^2)\):
\(E=\tfrac12m\,e^{-2\alpha t}\big[2\alpha^2(A_0+A_1t)^2+A_1^2-2\alpha A_1(A_0+A_1t)\big]\)

Quality factor \(Q=\frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma}=\frac{m\omega_0}{b}=\frac{\sqrt{mk}}{b}\approx2\pi\frac{E}{|\Delta E|_{cycle}}\). Critical: Q = ½. Log decrement \(\ln\frac{x(t)}{x(t+T_d)}=\gamma T_d\). Amplitude halves at \(t=\frac{2m\ln2}{b}\).

Driven (forced) oscillator

\(m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=F_0\cos\omega t\). x = transient (the damped solution, dies out) + steady state \(A\cos(\omega t-\delta)\). It ends up oscillating at the driving ω, not ω₀.

  1. \(\theta=\omega t-\delta\): \(x=A\cos\theta\), \(\dot x=-A\omega\sin\theta\), \(\ddot x=-A\omega^2\cos\theta\)
  2. Plug in: \(A(k-m\omega^2)\cos\theta-bA\omega\sin\theta=F_0\cos\omega t\)
  3. Expand: \(\cos\theta=\cos\omega t\cos\delta+\sin\omega t\sin\delta\), \(\sin\theta=\sin\omega t\cos\delta-\cos\omega t\sin\delta\)
  4. No sin ωt on the RHS: \((k-m\omega^2)\sin\delta-b\omega\cos\delta=0\) gives \(\tan\delta=\frac{b\omega}{k-m\omega^2}\) (phase lag)
  5. cos ωt: \(A[(k-m\omega^2)\cos\delta+b\omega\sin\delta]=F_0\). Use \(\sin\delta=\frac{b\omega}{D}\), \(\cos\delta=\frac{k-m\omega^2}{D}\):
\(A=\dfrac{F_0}{\sqrt{(k-m\omega^2)^2+(b\omega)^2}}\)\(=\dfrac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+4\gamma^2\omega^2}}\)

⑤ STANDING WAVES review p.6 · lect. 6

Two identical waves going opposite ways: \(y_1=A\sin(kx-\omega t)\), \(y_2=A\sin(kx+\omega t)\). Identity \(\sin a+\sin b=2\sin\frac{a+b}{2}\cos\frac{a-b}{2}\):

\(y(x,t)=\underbrace{2A\sin(kx)}_{\text{amp. depends on }x}\underbrace{\cos(\omega t)}_{\text{oscillation in time}}\)

Traveling \(A\sin(kx-\omega t)\): space and time mixed, the pattern moves. Standing: space and time separate, the pattern does not propagate.

Nodes (\(A_{local}=|2A\sin kx|=0\)): \(kx=n\pi\), so \(x_n=n\frac{\lambda}{2}\). Antinodes (|sin kx|=1, amp. 2A): \(x=\frac{\lambda}{4},\frac{3\lambda}{4},…\) Node–node = λ/2, node–antinode = λ/4. Points in one loop move in phase; neighbouring loops are opposite. The string is flat everywhere when cos ωt = 0 (all energy kinetic then).

%%STAND%%
Fixed both ends (both ends nodes; only some λ fit):
\(\lambda_n=\frac{2L}{n},\quad f_n=\frac{nv}{2L}=\frac{n}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{F_T}{\mu}}\), n = 1, 2, 3…
n=1 is the fundamental (1st harmonic, λ = 2L). \(f_n=nf_1\). Mode n has n loops, n antinodes, n+1 nodes (incl. ends).
Fixed–free (free end = antinode): \(\lambda_n=\frac{4L}{n}\), \(f_n=\frac{nv}{4L}\), odd n only. Free–free: same as fixed–fixed.

Guitar: tuning peg \(F_T\!\uparrow\Rightarrow f\!\uparrow\) (tighter means higher pitch). Thicker string \(\mu\!\uparrow\Rightarrow f\!\downarrow\). Fret \(L\!\downarrow\Rightarrow f\!\uparrow\). Scaling \(f\propto\frac{\sqrt{F_T}}{L\sqrt\mu}\): 4×F_T gives 2×f; 4×μ gives f/2; L/2 gives 2f (octave).

Two adjacent resonances given? Fixed–fixed: \(f_1=f_{n+1}-f_n\) and \(n=f_n/f_1\). Fixed–free: the gap is \(2f_1\). Wave vs. string: v is set by the string (\(F_T,\mu\)); f is set by the source/mode; λ = v/f.

Energy in a standing wave

\(u=\tfrac12\mu(\tfrac{\partial y}{\partial t})^2+\tfrac12F_T(\tfrac{\partial y}{\partial x})^2\) (KE + stretch PE). For \(y=A\sin kx\cos\omega t\), using \(F_Tk^2=\mu\omega^2\) (since \(v^2=F_T/\mu=\omega^2/k^2\)):

\(u=\tfrac12\mu A^2\omega^2[\sin^2\!kx\,\sin^2\!\omega t+\cos^2\!kx\,\cos^2\!\omega t]\)
  • Node (sin kx = 0): doesn't move, so KE density = 0. |cos kx| = 1, so the slope and PE density are max. Mostly PE.
  • Antinode: max displacement and max speed (at different times); slope 0.
  • L = integer × λ/2: \(\int_0^L\sin^2kx\,dx=\int_0^L\cos^2kx\,dx=\frac L2\), so \(E=\tfrac14\mu A^2\omega^2L\) is constant in time (KE and PE slosh locally).
  • \(E_n=\tfrac14\mu LA_n^2\omega_n^2\), \(\omega_n=\frac{n\pi v}{L}\), so \(E_n\propto n^2\) at the same antinode amplitude.
  • \(P_{avg}=0\): two equal and opposite traveling waves carry opposite energy fluxes. Energy is stored but not transported. Like a massive particle at rest (\(E_0=mc^2\), zero momentum); a traveling wave is like a massless one.

Why PE = ½F_T(∂y/∂x)²: \(ds=\sqrt{dx^2+dy^2}\approx dx[1+\tfrac12(\tfrac{\partial y}{\partial x})^2]\), extra length \(d\ell=\tfrac12(\tfrac{\partial y}{\partial x})^2dx\), work \(F_Td\ell\). PE depends on local slope, not height.

%%LJ%%

Lennard-Jones \(U=4\epsilon\big[(\tfrac{\sigma}{x})^{12}-(\tfrac{\sigma}{x})^{6}\big]\). The \(x^{-12}\) term is strongly repulsive (electron clouds overlap, Pauli exclusion). The \(-x^{-6}\) term is attractive (fluctuating dipoles, van der Waals).

  • \(U(\sigma)=0\).  \(\frac{dU}{dx}=4\epsilon(-12\sigma^{12}x^{-13}+6\sigma^6x^{-7})=0\) gives \(-2\sigma^6+x_0^6=0\), so \(x_0=2^{1/6}\sigma\approx1.12\sigma\)
  • \(U(x_0)=4\epsilon(\tfrac14-\tfrac12)=-\epsilon\): well depth = binding (dissociation) energy
  • \(U''=4\epsilon(156\tfrac{\sigma^{12}}{x^{14}}-42\tfrac{\sigma^6}{x^8})\), so \(U''(x_0)=\frac{72\epsilon}{x_0^2}=\frac{72\epsilon}{2^{1/3}\sigma^2}\approx57\frac{\epsilon}{\sigma^2}\) and \(\omega=\sqrt{\frac{72\epsilon}{\mu x_0^2}}\) (μ = reduced mass, m/2 for identical atoms)
  • \(F=\frac{24\epsilon}{x}[2(\tfrac{\sigma}{x})^{12}-(\tfrac{\sigma}{x})^6]\): repulsive for \(x<x_0\). E≥0 means unbound. Asymmetric well (anharmonic): average x grows with E, i.e. thermal expansion.

★ TRAVELING WAVES ch.3 · lect. 5 · disc. wk 3

Mechanical waves need a medium and carry energy and momentum, not mass. Transverse: medium moves ⊥ to propagation. Longitudinal: ∥. P-wave: longitudinal, faster, through solids and liquids. S-wave: transverse, slower, solids only.

\(y=A\sin(kx-\omega t+\phi)\) moves +x; \(kx+\omega t\) moves −x (constant phase gives x = vt + c). \(k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\), \(\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}\), \(v=\frac{\lambda}{T}=\lambda f=\frac{\omega}{k}\). Any \(f(x-vt)\) works. Pulse: Δx = vΔt.

Particle velocity \(v_y=\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=-A\omega\cos(kx-\omega t)\), max \(A\omega\) (≠ wave speed!). \(a_y=-\omega^2y\). At a crest the particle has \(v_y=0\) while the crest itself moves at v.

Wave eq. \(\frac{\partial^2y}{\partial x^2}=\frac1{v^2}\frac{\partial^2y}{\partial t^2}\). It's linear, so superposition holds. (A wave plus a 2ω wave going the other way still solves it with the same v, but has no single direction.)

String speed: \(F_{net,y}=F_T[(\frac{\partial y}{\partial x})_2-(\frac{\partial y}{\partial x})_1]=\mu\Delta x\frac{\partial^2y}{\partial t^2}\). Divide by \(F_T\Delta x\), let Δx→0: \(\frac{\partial^2y}{\partial x^2}=\frac{\mu}{F_T}\frac{\partial^2y}{\partial t^2}\), so \(v=\sqrt{F_T/\mu}\). Generally \(v=\sqrt{\frac{\text{restoring}}{\text{inertia}}}\); sound \(v=\sqrt{B/\rho}\).

Energy/power: \(E_\lambda=\tfrac12\mu A^2\omega^2\lambda,\ P_{avg}=\tfrac12\mu A^2\omega^2v\) \(=\tfrac12\omega^2A^2\sqrt{\mu F_T}\). Scalings: 2×A gives 4×P; 2×f gives 4×P; 4×F_T gives 2×v and 2×P.
Disc. wk 3: 4×μ (A, ω, F_T fixed): v and λ halve, but \(E_\lambda\) and \(P\) double (∝√μ). 4×F_T: v and λ double, so \(E_\lambda\) and P double.
Intensity \(I=P/\text{area}\). Sphere: \(I=\frac{P}{4\pi r^2}\), so \(A\propto\frac1r\). 2D circle: \(I=\frac{P}{2\pi r}\), so \(A\propto\frac1{\sqrt r}\).
